Problem Statement
Customers in Switzerland lack a digital, self-service advisory channel for life insurance. Pax — an established Swiss life insurer with a fast-growing product portfolio — offers advice exclusively through traditional human advisors. Insurance is complex, and customers often do not know which risks they should insure, how much they need to provide for, which products fit their situation, or what the difference between the various solutions is. At the same time, AI is changing how people search for information, make decisions, and interact with companies. Pax wants to know what the insurance advisor of the future can look like. The challenge is therefore: build the future of insurance advice. Develop a working prototype of an AI Insurance Advisor that guides a customer through their personal pension situation and derives comprehensible recommendations for suitable Pax products. We are not looking for an ordinary chatbot. We are looking for an idea that shows us what a digital pension advisor would look like if it were really good.

Context
A “digital advisor of the future” must put the customer's holistic life situation at the center, not the products:

Linking 1st, 2nd, and 3rd pension pillars (state/occupational/private)
Considering individual life goals (e.g., starting a family, home ownership, early retirement, child insurance)
Understanding the personal financial starting position
Products stay deliberately in the background and are only positioned secondarily as the fitting solution within the overall picture
Target Audience
Private customers of Pax in Switzerland — individuals (or couples/families) with a personal financial situation and life goals who want self-service advisory on life insurance / pension provision across all three pillars.

Must-haves
Capture needs, understand life situations (incl. financial situation), and derive development goals
Model a holistic pension logic across all three pillars
Enable individual life simulations (scenarios: family, home ownership, retirement)
Recommend situationally fitting Pax solutions (as a secondary step)
Cover the complete end-to-end flow: Advisory → Recommendation → Conclusion/Contract → Policy in the customer portal
The advisor understands the situation, asks intelligent follow-up questions, identifies relevant risks, structures the pension need, proposes suitable solutions, explains its recommendations, and shows possible next steps. It also recognizes when a human advisor is necessary.
Nice-to-haves
Bonus points are awarded for multilingual support (DE/FR/IT/EN), voice control, an avatar, simulation of future conceivable life situations, NPS feedback, and insights to Product Management on user drop-offs.

Resources
You receive real Pax product data (e.g. https://www.pax.ch/de/private-vorsorge) and documentation such as the advisory concept, access to the Logismata API (Swagger UI) for calculation functions such as pension situation and taxes (access still to be clarified), a concrete study case based on a real example, and an on-site Pax domain expert — Cem and Claude, on site Friday and Sunday.

No-Gos
Product-first selling is out — recommendations must never drive the conversation before the life situation is understood. Ignoring any of the three pillars or the full financial picture is not allowed, and anything that isn't a functional, working prototype won't qualify. Data-protection and privacy requirements (DSG/FADP compliance) must never be breached.

Follow-Up
The winning team gets to develop the Proof of Concept further together with the Pax innovation team, subject to agreement and feasibility. Possible next steps include working-student positions and Bachelor's or Master's theses.

Legal Framework
Customer, financial, and health data must be processed transparently with advance notice, and never sent to external AI models or cloud services uncontrolled. Every AI recommendation must be explainable, documented, and auditable on the basis of traceable criteria. In complex or high-impact cases, human review must remain possible and must not be replaced by the advisor. The implementation must be secure from day one — access protection, logging, and encryption — and must comply with insurance law, contractual duties (information, formal requirements), and anti-money-laundering rules.
